# SDLC (Software Development Life Cycle)

An introduction to the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC), engineering delivery models, DevOps methodologies, and the CALMS framework.

---

## 1. What is the SDLC?

The **Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC)** is a structured process used by development teams to design, develop, test, and maintain high-quality software systems. Depending on the methodology (Waterfall, Agile, Spiral), an SDLC framework typically comprises **6–8 phases**.

---

## 2. SDLC Phases

1. **Planning / Feasibility Stage:** Evaluating resource availability, technical viability, cost estimates, and business objectives.
2. **Requirements Definition:** Gathering functional and technical requirements from stakeholders to determine initial prototypes.
3. **Design & Prototyping:** Outlining user interface wireframes, architectural topologies, and network requirements.
4. **Software Development / Implementation:** Writing the actual production code.
5. **Testing & Quality Assurance:** Security assessments, unit tests, and integration testing.
6. **Deployment:** Pushing release packages to staging and production environments.
7. **Operations and Maintenance:** Managing bug fixes, updates, and monitoring production errors reported by end-users.

---

## 3. DevOps & the CALMS Framework

DevOps culture bridges engineering development and operational deployment through the **CALMS** framework:
- **C**ulture
- **A**utomation
- **L**ean
- **M**easurement
- **S**haring
